# Production Blueprint: Shrinking Pockets in a Big City (Eroding-Bandwidth Blues)

*Erosion & Daily Grit — 80 BPM · ≈ 3:42 · 74 bars · first chorus at 0:48*

## 1. Header & Style DNA
- **Title:** Shrinking Pockets in a Big City
- **ACE Layer 4 Context:** Erosion, as arrangement. Everything gets smaller every time it comes round: the verses lose bars (12, then 10, then 8), the choruses lose bars (8, 6, 4), the hook loses notes (five, four, three), the spectral city-wide refrain loses voices (eight, four, one), the lowest note on the record climbs out of reach (E1, E2, E3, then none), and the top end is rolled off step by step until the song sounds like it is playing from a phone speaker. The twelve-bar blues is the working man’s calendar, and it is losing days.

  The solo is the one place the arrangement goes the other way: it begins in the phone speaker — a single harmonica, mono, the smallest sound in the song — and swells out into the full-range roar of the city, then is cut back to the smallest version of the song for the coda. The reserved escalation is that full-bandwidth moment, spent once. Registers matter more here than anywhere: because each section has less room, each part must own its slot, and the harmonica takes the lead because it is the only instrument that still sounds like a person.
- **Production Style DNA:** bleak urban blues fusion, 80 BPM grinding dragging shuffle, E Phrygian with jazz-blues extensions, shrinking twelve-bar blues form, harmonica lead, bandwidth narrowing to a phone speaker, dwindling crowd refrain, shutter-slam and subway-brake percussion, distorted slide cello, moonglow convolution reverb, raw baritone and contralto

| **Drums** | Kit: SLOW BEAT = Drumaxx → Slow Beat MC (shuffle body, re-gridded to triplets); METALLIC = Drumaxx → Metallic SPYRO and Industrial 01–06 patterns; drops add Toxic Biohazard → Industrial Hazard ToTc (HAZARD, the strike) and SubWoofer Bomb ToTc (BOMB, the boom). Industrial drops are shop-shutter slams (METALLIC + HAZARD) and subway-brake squeals (HAZARD pitched down and filtered): sparse, irregular, and getting quieter with the mix. |
| **Vocal Treatment** | **Hoarse Worker → Platform Crowd.** Baritone raw, close, mono; contralto 20% left, answering from outside (low-passed, as if through a door). The spectral choir is the city: 8 → 4 → 1 copies across the choruses and 12 at the solo’s peak, with platform noise inside it. Designed power moments: the last line of Chorus 1 and the solo’s wave. No ad-libs: this song never gets to shout, and the coda is spoken. |
| **Clarity Strategy** | Vocal Void (2–4 kHz) stays open: Maximus’ 2–4 kHz band on CELLO, HARP, DARK ORCH and MOON is keyed from the vocal bus (−3 to −5 dB while the voice sings). Register map: SUB 30–60 Hz · UPRIGHT 60–200 Hz · CELLO growl 80–250 Hz, cry 600 Hz–1.5 kHz · HARP 400 Hz–1.8 kHz · STALKER 150–800 Hz · CHOIR PAD 300 Hz–1 kHz · MOON and strings air above 5 kHz. Two parts never share a register at the same moment — they alternate in time. Here the vocal void is held by narrowing rather than notching: each erosion step removes highs and lows around the voice. Erosion is done with master-bus stock EQ/filters (utility, not a palette choice). |

- **Central question:** Somebody’s getting the difference — who?
